# Farm design study (from ref3 upper-right; director: reference is authority)

## Agricultural language (not objects — relationships)
- LONG barn (2:1), blue gabled roof, OPEN working side (dark interior + stored
  goods visible), timber frame, stone base, big gable doors. The barn is a
  working machine, not a house: length, openness, and yard matter more than trim.
- Thatch farmhouses (tan ragged roofs, mud/timber, veranda) — residential
  vocabulary RENDERED POOR on purpose: small, secondary to the barn.
- Glass greenhouse: white frame rhythm + gabled glass, crops visible inside.
- Fields are DIFFERENTIATED systems: gold wheat blocks (dense, tall, yellow),
  green vegetable rows (dotted dark-green rows on soil), tilled beds (brown
  ridge lines, empty-but-ready). Fenced per-plot (post+rail), dirt paths between,
  grain stacks (cream cones) at the barn.
- Windmill OVER the fields on the knoll (tower/cap/axle/hub/blades coherent),
  plus small water-tower on legs near plots.
- Farmyard: packed-earth work apron between barn doors and plots.
- Material split from civic/residential: weathered timber > plaster, blue roof
  shared with civic (island-wide built color), thatch only on farmhouses,
  soil/gold/green crop triad, NO flowers in fields (weeds are grass).

## Motifs to repeat (agricultural construction language)
post+rail fences, ridge-line soil beds, open-sided sheds, raised stone bases,
gable vents, sliding barn doors, grain-stack cones, tank-on-legs, windbreak walls.
